# AUDIT: 1-bedroom listings in Bloom Towers, JVC (Property Finder)

**Checked:** 3 Nov 2026, 15:20–15:55 GST, on propertyfinder.ae
**Sample:** 30 listings, 15 for rent and 15 for sale. Each is the first 15 shown in Property Finder's default order, which puts paid placements first. That is what a searcher actually sees.
**Comparison base:** every 1BR listing live in the building at the time: **170 for rent and 38 for sale**. Price per sqft for each listing is compared with the building median for the same furnishing.

Rent prices run from AED 58,500 to 100,000 a year (median 69,999). Sale prices run from AED 839,999 to 1,200,000 (median 950,000).

Medians count **listings, not unique flats**. In this first table, "unfurnished" means everything not marked furnished, which includes 3 "partly furnished" rent listings. **The furnished sale median is sensitive:** it moves from 1,358 to 1,412 once confirmed duplicate copies are removed. So every furnished-sale percentage in the table below is approximate (see "Benchmark check").

## How I ran the audit
I chose Bloom Towers because one building with enough rent and sale stock lets me compare like with like. I went through the listings against a fixed set of checks: price against comparables, repeat listings of the same flat, photos, the permit, availability signals, and anything else wrong. Claude ran the same checks as a second pass in my browser, and also ran the counts across all 208 listings and the side-by-side photo comparisons. I then ran a quality-control review, with ChatGPT as a second reader, and corrected wherever the reading went beyond the evidence: a repeat listing doesn't show an agency acted improperly, shared photos can be shared marketing, and "Lowest Price in Market" is unsupported rather than false. Every change is in the corrections log.

## How each listing was checked
- Each listing page was opened and the following was recorded: price, size, furnishing, cheques, agent, agency, badges, placement level, listed date, "available from" date, the permit validation link, the title and the description.
- The first 3 photos of each listing were compared side by side wherever two listings could be the same flat.
- **Duplicates** are only marked "confirmed" when the photos match. A matching size alone does not count: the same layout repeats on every floor.
- **Permits:** the audit checked whether the page shows a Trakheesi (Dubai Land Department) validation link. It did **not** open those links or validate the permits themselves.
- **Availability:** the audit can only report signals from the page (dates, "vacant" or "tenanted" in the text). Whether a flat is actually still available is **unknown** for every listing; that would need the agent's answer.

## Verdict key
- **Trust:** no problems found in what a searcher can see.
- **Check:** something a searcher should ask about before relying on it. A **pricing anomaly** (well above or below comparables) is always a Check, never "misleading" on its own: price alone doesn't show deception.
- **Duplicate:** the same flat appears in more than one listing, confirmed by matching photos.
- **Misleading:** clear evidence that this specific listing misrepresents something. After the quality-control pass, **no listing meets this bar on its own** (see the corrections log).

## What Property Finder's "Verified" badge means
**What PF says it verifies** (from PF's own pages):
- Verification is per listing. PF's quality-control team reviews documents for that listing. In Dubai these are Form A, the title deed, the owner's passport or Emirates ID, and the property advertising agreement.
- Verification lasts 45 days for residential rentals and 90 days for residential sales. Renewal needs valid documents and the property still on the market.
- PF's partner page doesn't list price as something it checks. A 2019 PF blog post, updated in 2024, says the aim includes avoiding misinformation on "price, area and availability", but doesn't explain how.

**What we can reasonably infer:** at the time of its last verification, documents connecting this listing to an owner's authorisation were reviewed and accepted. We don't know when that was for any particular listing. A badge may have been renewed at any point within its window.

**What the badge doesn't establish, on the evidence here:**
- **That the listing is the only one for the flat.** Each agency's listing is verified separately; see #21/#25/#26.
- **That the price is consistent across listings.** See #19/#29.
- **That the photos show the flat.** See #22/#23.
- **That the flat is available today.**

None of this means verification is misleading or ineffective. **Verification and transaction readiness answer different questions.**

## Findings

**Verdict count (30 listings, after quality control):** Trust 12 · Check 13 · Duplicate 5 (2 flats) · Misleading 0.

Each finding is labelled with its evidence base:
- **[Sample]:** the 30 listings checked one by one. These are the first 15 results in PF's default order, which is what searchers see first. They aren't a random sample, so they don't generalise to all 208 listings.
- **[Building]:** counts over every 1BR listing live in Bloom Towers on 3 Nov 2026 (170 rent, 38 sale). These are counts of what listings say. They don't check whether it's true.

1. **[Sample] The same flat appears several times, sometimes at different prices. Confirmed by matching photos.** One flat is listed by 3 agencies (#21, #25, #26: AED 949,999 / 950,000 / 950,000). Another is listed by 2 agencies, **AED 40,000 apart** (#19 at 960,000, #29 at 1,000,000). So 5 of the 15 sale listings checked are 2 flats. All 5 carry "Verified". That's consistent with how the badge works (per-listing documents, not one record per flat), so this is not proof of anything fake. The searcher's problem is noise and conflicting prices.
2. **[Sample] Look-alike listings are usually different flats.** Photos were compared for every look-alike group: same size and tower, or same agent and price. Only 2 groups were the same flat. These were different flats with the same layout: #5/#6, #8/#9/#10, the four 673 sqft flats in Tower B, and 166525470 vs #19/#29. Matching on size, tower or price alone would wrongly flag honest agents.
3. **[Sample] Photos can't always be relied on to show the advertised flat.** #22 and #23 (same agency) use one identical photo set for two listings that state different sizes (722 and 697 sqft) and describe themselves as specific units. Neither says the photos are representative. This is a strong integrity concern. It is **not proven** misrepresentation: the photos could be a shared marketing shoot, and I can't tell which listing, if either, is inaccurate. The same pattern shows up on the rent side (finding 11).
4. **[Sample] Holiday-home listings sit among yearly leases with prices that aren't comparable.** #4 and #7 are holiday-home lets: DTCM permits, "annual rental price includes utility bills", plus a booking fee, VAT, tourism tax and deposit on top. Both are paid "Featured" placements. #7 has been live about 23 months, but the page only says "Listed more than 6 months ago".
5. **[Sample] Price outliers in this building are above the market, not below it.** The unusual prices were rent at +20% (#1), +21% (#4, explained by bills) and +25% (#11), and sale at +10% (#24) and +12% (#27). None of the cheapest listings in the sample was below the building's normal range. These are **pricing anomalies, not evidence of deception**. The "too cheap to be true" bait in the brief **did not show up** in this building's 1BR sample. That doesn't mean it doesn't happen elsewhere.
6. **[Sample] There are unsupported claims in titles.** #16's "Lowest Price in Market" has no stated scope. In this building, 8 of 38 1BR sale listings were cheaper, and its price per sqft is the median.
7. **[Building] Common listing claims carry little information.** 104 of 208 listings mention "high floor" (82 in the title). Only 2 mention a low floor, and both are inside keyword tag lists. 20 of 208 claim a Marina view (16 in the title), from JVC; another 40 mention the Marina only as a nearby location.
8. **[Building] Paid placement and verification: an association, not a cause.** 3 of the 7 paid rent listings are not verified, against 9 of 163 standard rent listings. That's a large difference on a very small paid group, and 2 of those 3 are holiday homes, where the verification process may not apply. It says nothing about why. It does mean a searcher's first results aren't necessarily the most checked.
9. **[Building] Long-running rentals are common, and their age is hidden.** 39 of 170 rent listings (23%) were listed before August 2026. On the listing page, any age past 6 months shows only as "more than 6 months ago". A long-running listing is a reason to ask, not proof the flat is gone.
10. **[Sample] Some fields contradict the photos or title.** #30 says unfurnished, but the photos show furniture (possibly the tenant's; unknown). #3 states cheques in its title, but the cheques field is empty. #2 names no individual agent (PF labels it "Direct From Developer").
11. **[Building, rent] Repeated rent listings: some match on photos, but the evidence is weaker than for sales.** See "Repeated rent listings" below. Of 24 repeated combinations of tower, size, price and furnishing, 7 show the same photos across the listings, which suggests the same flat. In 5 of those 7, one of the agencies is the same agency, and in 4 the same two agencies appear together. One of those agencies also uses one photo set on two different sizes, which weakens photo matching as proof.

## Repeated rent listings: what the "repeats" are
A **repeat** here means two or more listings with the same tower, size, price and furnishing.
- **Building-wide (165 standard rent listings):** 24 such combinations, covering **53 listings**. That's 29 listings beyond the first in each combination.
- **Who is listing them:** 3 combinations come from a single agency each; the other 21 involve two or more agencies.
- **Photo check:** the first 2 photos of every listing in all 24 combinations were compared.

**Why even the photo matches are weaker evidence on the rent side:**
- In 5 of the 7 photo-matched combinations, one of the agencies is the same agency (K D K Real Estate). In 4, the same two agencies (K D K and Alarfeen) appear together.
- In a separate combination (C-673 at 63,000), K D K's listing uses the **same photos as the C-667 flat**, but states a different size.

**This is an observation, not an allegation:** agencies co-marketing a landlord's portfolio would produce exactly this pattern.

**Bottom line:** I can't say the rental market is inflated by 29 duplicates. I can say that 7 repeated combinations show matching photos, and at most 8 listings would disappear if those are the same flats.
